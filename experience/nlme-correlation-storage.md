`corCompSymm` keeps the constrained `Rho` until `Initialize`, then stores `log((Rho - inf)/(1 - Rho))` with `inf = -1/(maxLen - 1)`. `corLin` and `corSpher` store `log(range - minD)` and add `minD` back. `corRatio` stores `log(range)` only. `corCAR1` is a logit, not the `corAR1` map. `corExp` keeps the supplied range until `Initialize`, and unconstrained `coef` still applies `exp` to that storage. `corNatural` constrained `coef` needs `maxCov` from `Initialize`. `pdDiag` stores `log(diag(crossprod(chol))) / 2`. `pdFactor` `diag` of a vector is `np.diag(exp(values))`; NumPy's second argument is an offset. `varPower` weights are `abs(covariate ** (-coef))`. `varIdent` methods named `object` hide the builtin; use `dtype=np.dtype("O")`. `getCovariateFormula` drops a bar only when it is the root. `ARMA` unconstrained coefficients use `(1-exp(-u))/(1+exp(-u))` after the exponential step. `fdHess` completes `Hess + t(Hess)` and still misses the analytic Hessian by about 1e-6. `balancedGrouped` tie breaks follow factor level order. `simulate.lme` still calls R until `lme` is native. `LDEsysMat` indexes `pars` with 1-based `Par`. `Dim` for `corSpatial` replaces the third entry with `spClass` and starts each group at the cumulative pair count, not the row count. `corSymm` `Initialize` fills the lower triangle column by column, not row by row (the two orders match only up to `maxCov` 3), and its angles come from R's upper `chol`, so numpy's lower factor must be transposed first. `corNatural` `Initialize` stores the transpose of the block that `_symm_fact` returns (R's `nat_factList` holds it), and the match is 5.6e-17 only after that transpose; without it the factor differs by 0.31.
`corARMA` stores its coefficients unconstrained (`ARMA_untransPar` maps them back), and `Initialize` builds `factor` as `U^{-T}` from the upper Cholesky `U` of the ARMA correlation (`ARMA_fact`), with factor logDet `-sum(log|U_ii|)`. The stored `logDet` is the negative of that factor logDet, so `logDet` returns the same half log-determinant that R does; `recalc` then multiplies the `Xy` block per group by the factor, and the match is about 1e-15 for ARMA(2,1) and MA(1).
