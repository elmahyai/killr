`fit_gnls` (nlme `src/gnls.c`, crate `gnlsfit`) writes the convergence code, the iteration count, and the weighted residual sum of squares into `settings[3]`, `settings[4]`, and `settings[5]`. The model callback returns column-major `[gradient | residuals]`, and the Gauss–Newton step stays in pivoted column order. When that residual already lies in the column space of the gradient, the convergence criterion is zero and the step is not applied. `glsEstimate` still calls `gls_estimate` in `src/nlmefit.c`. With no variance parameters, `gnlsApVar` returns NULL.

`symm_recalc` and `nat_recalc` read a covariate already shifted to start at 0. `pdims` is `unlist(Dim)`: `N`, `M`, `maxLen`, `sumLenSq`, then `len`, then `start`. `start[i]` offsets both that covariate and the rows of column-major `Xy`. The factor is `dtrsl` job 11 on nlme `chol`, and that `chol` is R's `dpofa` with the `1e-14` cutoff. `AR1_recalc` and `compSymm_recalc` overwrite the unconstrained parameter and fill a factor that `R_Calloc` left zero. `R_forceSymbols` is set, so `.C` needs the symbol from the nlme namespace.

`lme` minimises the leading Koshal intercept from `mixed_loglik` with `gradHess` on. That surface is flat: a Newton step that drives the returned gradient to zero can sit about `1e-6` away in the variance parameters from `nlminb`'s relative-convergence stop, while the fixed effects still agree. R's `nlminb` call passes no gradient, so PORT takes forward differences of logLik (step `sqrt(eps)*max(abs(x),1)`) and its first trial is the steepest-descent step of length 1 (identity model Hessian), where the Python dogleg uses the analytic Hessian; the shrink rule (`max(0.1, ...)`, quadratic fraction) does match R, but the proposal does not, so the path diverges at step 1. `pdSymm` coefficients have to be mapped with `generate_dm_half` (`factor_from_parameters`); a Python `pdFactor` reconstruction does not match the kernel.
