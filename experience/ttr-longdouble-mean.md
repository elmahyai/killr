An `apply` mean is a long-double sum. R's `sum`, `colSums`, and `rowSums` also accumulate in long double, so NumPy needs `np.longdouble` accumulation, cast to double after the sum, to reproduce R's bits. Without it, ill-conditioned GP paths drift from R at about 1e-9 and can miss 1e-8. The same applies to `scale()`, where `colMeans` and `sum(v^2)` are long double. When a merge order turns on a near-tie, one ulp in `scale` or in `traceW` (the Fortran `mcltrw`) flips it, so call `mcltrw` from the kernel crate instead of NumPy.
