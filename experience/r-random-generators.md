A matching seed number is not R's stream. Use the ported generators: `norm_rand` packs two 27-bit uniforms, `exp_rand` is Ahrens-Dieter, and `runif` is `unif_rand`. Put the same literal sample in both proofs when the function does not draw.
Default `rnorm` packs two uniforms. The Fortran `unifrnd` is `unif_rand`. SciPy's cdf does not follow that sequence. `rmvt` draws the normal matrix first and the chi-square second.
`sample(k, n, replace = TRUE, prob = p)` heapsorts `p` with R's `revsort` before the cumulative draw, so ties keep R's order, and `rnorm` with `sd = 0` consumes no uniform. Both are checked against R for mclust `sim*` (`packages/mclust/proof/sim-rng-r.R`).
`sample.int(n, size)` without replacement is `R_unif_index` over `unif_rand`. Take `bits = ceil(log2(n))`, build 16-bit chunks from `floor(unif_rand() * 65536)` up to `bits`, mask to `bits`, and redraw while the value is `>= n`. For `n > 1e7` with `size <= n/2`, R's `sample2` redraws until the value is new. NumPy's `choice` does not follow this. `packages/permute/permute/_rng.py` follows it and matches R for `set.seed(42); sample.int(10, 10)` and for the hash path, checked by `packages/permute/proof/rng-check-r.R`.
